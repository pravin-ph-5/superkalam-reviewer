z Q. “Federalism in India is a product of both historical circumstances and constitutional design.” Examine.

Indian federalism has evolved through both historical circumstances and constitutional design.

The Government of India Act, 1935 provided an institutional background for the distribution of powers. The experience of Partition and the need for national integration also shaped the Constituent Assembly's preference for a strong Union.

The Constitution establishes a strong Union through the Union, State and Concurrent Lists, residuary powers and emergency provisions.Value addition
Could mention relevant Articles / Schedules.

At the same time, states have constitutionally protected areas of authority and institutions such as the Finance Commission support fiscal federalism. The GST Council is another example of cooperative federalism.

Recent debates around gubernatorial powers and fiscal transfers show that Indian federalism continues to evolve.Analysis
Push the argument further by linking design to political practice.

Therefore, Indian federalism reflects both constitutional choices and the country's historical-political context. Its future depends on balancing national unity with meaningful state autonomy.
